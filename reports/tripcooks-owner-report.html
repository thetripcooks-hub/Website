Briefing
Context

Here's what was wrong — and why it mattered.

When someone Googled "tripcooks", one of the first links they saw under your website was labelled "Hamburger-menu" — pointing to what looked like a technical page about a navigation menu, not a travel service. This was the most visible symptom of a deeper issue: Google simply didn't have enough information about your site to describe it accurately.

Think of it this way: Imagine opening a shop with no signs, no window display, and no description on the door. People walking past wouldn't know what you sell. That's what your website looked like to Google — and to anyone who found a link to it on social media.

The root cause was that every single page on your website — your home page, your trips, your about page, everything — was giving Google the exact same generic description. Google couldn't tell them apart. So it started guessing, and it guessed wrong.

On top of that, your website had no sitemap (a list of all your pages that you hand to Google), no directions telling Google which pages it should and shouldn't include in search results, and none of the special tags that make links look great when shared on WhatsApp or Instagram.

Work Completed

Everything that was fixed — in plain English.

🔍

Fixed the "Hamburger-menu" problem

Removed the technical label that was confusing Google and replaced it with a proper, meaningful description. This is the fix that will clear up what you saw in search results.

🏷️

Gave every page its own identity

Every page on your site — home, trips, about, contact, private trips — now has its own unique title and description that accurately tells Google what that page is about.

✈️

Made each trip page fully Google-ready

Every individual trip page (e.g. Paris, Bali) now automatically pulls its title, description, and photo from your content system and presents them properly to Google.

🗺️

Built a sitemap for Google

Created a complete list of all your pages and handed it to Google, so it knows exactly what to find and index on your site — including every trip you add in future.

🚦

Told Google what not to index

Your cart, checkout, and payment pages are now hidden from Google. These private pages were wasting Google's attention and occasionally appearing in search results where they shouldn't.

📱

Made links look great on social media

When someone shares a link to your site on WhatsApp, Instagram, or LinkedIn, it will now show a proper title, description, and — for trip pages — a photo of the destination.

📍

Set the official address of every page

Every page now has a "canonical URL" — its official address. This prevents Google from getting confused if the same page appears at slightly different web addresses.

⭐

Added structured data for trips

Each trip page now includes hidden information that Google can read to potentially show trip details (dates, prices, destinations) directly in search results without anyone clicking.

🏢

Registered Trip Cooks with Google's knowledge system

Added official organisation information that helps Google understand Trip Cooks as a brand — your name, website, Instagram, and TikTok — which can lead to a branded knowledge panel.

📄

Cleaned up thin/empty pages

Two pages (/privacy and /terms) were essentially blank — showing only a single word. These now redirect visitors to your full policy page, removing a quality concern for Google.

🤖

Set Google crawling rules

Created a robots.txt file — a set of instructions for search engine bots telling them exactly which parts of your site to visit and which to skip entirely.

📝

Wrote full project documentation

Created a complete technical README for the codebase so that any developer who works on this project in future can understand how everything is built without starting from scratch.

What Happens Next

What to expect over the next few weeks.

SEO improvements don't show results overnight — Google needs time to visit your site, read the changes, and update its index. Here's a realistic timeline of what you'll see and when.

Now — Deploy & Go Live

All the improvements are live

Once the updated code is deployed to tripcooks.tours, everything takes effect: correct page titles, social media previews, the sitemap, structured data for trips, and Google's crawling rules. Nothing is visible to users — these are behind-the-scenes improvements.

Days 1–3

Set up Google Search Console & submit the sitemap

This is the most important action you can take. Submitting the sitemap tells Google exactly where all your pages are and asks it to re-index everything. Think of it as formally introducing your website to Google.

Week 1–2

Google begins re-reading your pages

Google's bots will start visiting your updated pages and reading the new descriptions, titles, and structured data. Your trips will start appearing with correct titles in search results. You won't see the "Hamburger-menu" link for much longer.

Week 2–6

The "Hamburger-menu" sitelink disappears

Once Google re-crawls your home page and sees the proper organisation information, it will replace that incorrect sitelink with meaningful page names. This is the most visible improvement you'll notice in search results.

Month 1–2

Rich results may appear for trips

Google may begin showing trip details — destination, dates, price from — directly in search results without anyone needing to click your link first. This depends on Google deciding your structured data meets its standards.

Month 2 onwards

Organic growth through content (if started)

If a blog or travel guides section is launched, this is when new visitors — people who aren't looking for Trip Cooks specifically, but searching things like "group trip to Morocco" — will start finding your site for the first time.
